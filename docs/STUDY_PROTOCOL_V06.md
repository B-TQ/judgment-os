# v0.6 harder-task follow-up

The private, timestamped `study_plan.json` was written before any generation. This document describes that plan; results belong in a separate report.

Eight newly authored, self-contained fictional work tasks cover four cases with a natural mismatch between a reasonable optional domain default and the situation, two cases where the default fits, and two exact controls. The author received no core Skill, prior cases, outputs, condition labels or results. All eight are retained. Only representation errors or internal contradictions can be corrected before generation, with originals and a change record preserved. Difficulty is a design intention, not a measured fact before outcomes.

One attempt per case and condition yields 32 slots. A receives the task; B adds the domain Skill; C adds the unchanged full Judgment Preserver; D adds the same one-sentence reminder used since v0.3. Seed 20261028 determines order/IDs; native workspaces stage condition-specific Skill files. The requested model is `gpt-6.1-sol`, effort `low`, authenticated through the existing subscription with API-key environment variables removed. No rerolls, selected replacements, new defects disguised as genuine generations, or tuning the core against these outcomes.

The primary contrast is C versus D. Secondary contrasts are C versus B, D versus B and B versus A. Record failed/aborted/missing attempts rather than quietly dropping them. Inspect account quota after groups of four attempts; stop starting generations at 85% of the five-hour limit or 82% of the weekly limit. These shared account percentages cannot measure this project's exact cost. No purchase, reset redemption or separately billed API is authorized.

Two fresh model-review chats receive independently shuffled and re-keyed groups containing the same task's four final answers. They receive the task-specific quality anchors and public deterministic text-check results, but no treatment mapping, Skill text, generation prompts, model/runtime or history. They mark textual hard gates separately, give integer 0–4 quality scores only to passing answers, and compare all six pairs with concrete evidence. A passing answer beats a failed one; two failed answers tie for the pairwise analysis. Two passing answers tie unless the difference materially affects the user's achievable outcome. Pair judgments need not follow coarse integer scores. Same-task grouping reduces scale drift but introduces relative comparison and possible position effects; random ordering mitigates rather than eliminates those effects.

Review imports require complete rows, frozen packets and hashes, exact IDs, explicit model provenance, and consistency with deterministic failures and declared gates. Review identities cannot be described as independent humans. The scope is final-output text, not proof of all tool-use restrictions. Same-provider bias, ambient host instructions, unknown served model revision, synthetic cases and one attempt per condition remain limitations.

Report each judge's cases, gate decisions and preferences. With two judges, disagreement remains `disputed`; do not force a majority. Unanimity is concordance, not ground truth. Eight heterogeneous cases and an unknown served revision do not support population efficacy, statistical significance or efficacy confidence intervals. Judge rows are not additional independent task samples. Passing-only quality scores are selection-biased and exploratory.

The predeclared product decision threshold for making the full core a default is concordant material C-over-D preference on at least three of the four mismatch cases, no concordant C-under-D preference on the fit cases, and no additional control failure. This is a conservative adoption rule, not a hypothesis test. If unmet, keep the full core opt-in and prefer simpler task-specific practice. Do not rewrite the core until the evidence supports a concrete defect and a new held-out study is warranted.

Stop this iteration after the fixed batch, two complete reviews, disagreement inspection, usable guidance, integrity checks, clean release verification and a separately verified immutable backup. Future human utility assessment and cross-model replication remain future work rather than claims of completion.


## Post-generation isolation amendment (transparent deviation)

After the fixed 32 outputs and initial two reviews were collected, both reviewers disclosed that Project requirement-archive location tools had incidentally returned other-chat titles/summaries. They reported not using that information and did not open chat history, but the coordinator cannot verify the absence of influence. Initial reviews are preserved and their isolation claim is downgraded.

A timestamped amendment was recorded before two corrective reviews. The same 32 unchanged outputs receive new aliases/positions in two **projectless** chats, with known projectless status supplied directly. They may not read chat/project inventories, session indexes/global state, archive history or other context. There is no Project requirement archive to locate. They must disclose actual read scope and any incidental exposure. This improves the information boundary without claiming OS-level isolation or independent human judgment.

No generation is repeated and no case/core/rubric/adoption threshold changes. Initial judge01/02 and corrective judge03/04 are analyzed as separate phases, with original results known before this amendment; neither phase is selected for a favorable outcome. Additional ratings are repeated judgments on eight cases, not new experimental samples or cross-model replication. Report-subset selection and a regression test were added so phases need not be silently pooled. Prior script bytes remain in the pre-generation and pre-review checkpoints. The original input-freeze hash of that reporting script therefore describes its earlier version, while the generator inputs/core remain unchanged.
